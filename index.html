how it works

The tool

the telescope01

SPHEREx is NASA's all-sky infrared survey telescope, launched in March 2025. It maps the whole sky every six months in 102 colours between 0.75 and 5 microns, so every patch of sky is seen again and again.

the images02

For each patch the tool asks NASA/IPAC IRSA for every SPHEREx exposure, cuts out a small square, removes the glow of dust in our own solar system, and puts every cutout on one fixed grid with north up. Stars far away stay still. A star close to us walks.

the check03

We tested it on objects we already know. Across its 60 exposures, Barnard's Star lands on average within a tenth of a pixel of where Gaia says it should be. If the tool shows what we know is there, we can believe what it shows elsewhere.

The difference view subtracts the median of all images from each one. Whatever never changes turns flat grey. Something that moved leaves a bright spot where it is and a dark spot where it was.
