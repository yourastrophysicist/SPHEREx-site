SPHEREx

A small web tool for the part of the sky that moves. Type a position, and it plays the SPHEREx images of that patch one after another, like a flipbook, so anything that moved between visits jumps out.

what it will be

The plan

the telescope01

SPHEREx is NASA's all-sky infrared survey telescope, launched in March 2025. It maps the whole sky every six months in 102 colours between 0.75 and 5 microns, so every patch of sky gets visited again and again.

the tool02

You enter a right ascension and a declination. The tool fetches the public SPHEREx images of that patch from NASA/IPAC IRSA, puts them in time order, and plays them as a short animation. A nearby star or brown dwarf that moves across the sky shows up as a dot that jumps.

the check03

Before trusting it anywhere else, we test it on objects whose motion we already know, such as Barnard's Star, Epsilon Indi B, WISE 0855, RW Aurigae and R Draconis. If the tool shows what we know is there, we can believe what it shows elsewhere.

The challenge, Planet X and SPHEREx, also asks whether SPHEREx could catch a distant Planet X. Part of the project is saying honestly, with numbers, how faint is too faint.
